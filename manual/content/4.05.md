The CPU holds `A`, `D`, the ALU, `Decode` from 4.04, `Jump` from 4.05 and the
counter from 3.08. Its interface to the world outside is four signals to
memory and one from it.

```truth
.port | .direction | .meaning
`instruction` | in | the word the ROM holds at address `pc`
`inM` | in | the word the RAM holds at address `addressM`
`reset` | in | forces `pc` to 0
`addressM` | out | which RAM word `M` refers to
`outM` | out | the value to write there
`writeM` | out | whether to write it this tick
`pc` | out | the address of the next instruction
```

Feed `instruction` to `Decode`, then wire exactly this:

```truth
.input | .source
`A` register `in` | `Mux16(ALU out, instruction, sel = Not(op))`
`A` register `load` | `loadA`
`D` register `in` | ALU out
`D` register `load` | `loadD`
ALU `x` | `D` out
ALU `y` | `Mux16(A out, inM, sel = useM)`
ALU control | `zx nx zy ny f no`
`outM` | ALU out
`addressM` | `A` out
`writeM` | `writeM`
`PC in` | `A` out
`PC load` | `Jump(zr, ng, j1, j2, j3)`
`PC inc` | constant 1
`PC reset` | `reset`
```

Two of those lines carry the design.

**The mux on `A`'s input** is what lets one register serve both instruction
layouts. On a C-instruction `op` is 1, `Not(op)` is 0, and the mux selects the
ALU. On an A-instruction it selects the instruction word itself, whose top bit
is 0 and whose remaining 15 bits are the value.

**The mux on the ALU's `y` input** is what gives `M` meaning. `useM` is
`instruction[12]` unchanged, so the `a` bit selects between the `A` register's
output and the word the RAM is currently presenting.

`PC inc` is tied to a constant 1 and never changes. The counter from 3.08
gives `load` priority over `inc`, so a jump overrides the increment without
anything needing to switch `inc` off.

Build and test in signal order: ALU inputs correct first, then storage into
each of the three destinations, then jumps.

::: watch
Everything in this chip reads its inputs and writes its outputs within one
tick, which is only consistent because `A` and `D` are edge-triggered. When an
instruction writes `A` and reads `M` in the same tick, `M` is the word at the
*old* `A` — the new value does not reach `addressM` until the next tick. If
the registers were the transparent latches of 3.03, `addressM` would change
mid-tick and the RAM would be addressed by two different values during one
instruction.
:::
