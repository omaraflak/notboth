---
num: 4.05
title: Jumps
sig: Jump(zr, ng, j1, j2, j3) → load
needs: 4.02, 4.04
---

`Jump` decides whether the program counter loads instead of increments. Its
inputs are the ALU's two status outputs and the three gated jump bits from
4.04. Its single output goes to the counter's `load` input.

`zr` and `ng` describe the ALU output produced by the instruction currently
executing. They are mutually exclusive, so the positive case is the complement
of both:

```truth
.outcome | .expression
negative | `ng`
zero | `zr`
positive | `Not(Or(zr, ng))`
```

An instruction jumps when it requested an outcome that occurred. Since exactly
one outcome occurs, the three terms are disjoint and an Or combines them:

```
load = Or(Or(And(j1, ng),
             And(j2, zr)),
          And(j3, Not(Or(zr, ng))))
```

One Or and one Not for the positive test, three Ands, two Ors to combine:
seven gates.

`j1`, `j2` and `j3` arrive already conjoined with `op` in 4.04, so an
A-instruction cannot jump regardless of what its low three bits contain. Do
not gate them again here.

::: watch
`zr` and `ng` describe the ALU's output, not the contents of any register.
There is no path from a register to `Jump` that does not pass through the ALU.
Testing `D` therefore means computing `D` — the instruction `D;JGT` sets
`comp` to the expression `D`, stores the result nowhere, and jumps on the sign
of what the ALU produced.
:::
