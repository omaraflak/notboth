---
num: 3.07
title: RAM8
sig: RAM8(in[16], load, address[3], clk) → out[16]
needs: 3.06, 1.09, 1.10
---

Eight registers and an address that picks one of them.

- **address[3]** — which of the eight.
- **in[16]**, **load**, **clk** — write `in` into that register on the next
  edge, if `load` is high.
- **out[16]** — that register’s contents, always. Reading needs no clock;
  only writing waits for the edge.

Two jobs, and both parts are already on your shelf: route the write signal to
exactly one register (a demux) and select one register’s output to present (a
wide mux).

Build it and the recursion is visible: RAM64 is eight RAM8s addressed by three
more bits, RAM512 is eight RAM64s. Only the depth of the address changes.

Then stop. Having proved you can, use the built-in RAM part for the real
machine. It is worth saying why that is a reasonable place to stop: a
sixteen-thousand-word memory made of flip-flops is around two and a half
million gates, and real hardware does not build it that way either — an SRAM
cell is six transistors, not a netlist of gates. You have demonstrated that
memory *can* be made of NANDs. Now buy it off the shelf and spend your gates
on the CPU, where the interesting decisions are.
