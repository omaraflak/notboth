title: The ALU
sig: ALU(x[16], y[16], zx, nx, zy, ny, f, no) → out[16], zr, ng
needs: all of Part I, 2.03–2.05

The arithmetic and logic unit: one box that computes any of a couple of dozen
useful functions of two numbers, with control bits selecting which. Add,
subtract, negate, increment, and, or, pass one input through, produce a
constant — all from one chip.

Do not build a dozen circuits and mux between them. The whole menu comes from
three phases: *pre-condition* each input, perform *one* operation on the
results, then *post-condition* the answer. Six single-bit controls decide what
happens at each phase, and they act in the order the numbers move through the
chip.

Six single-bit controls, acting in the order the numbers move through the
chip:

- **zx** — replace `x` with zero.
- **nx** — invert `x`, after any zeroing.
- **zy** — replace `y` with zero.
- **ny** — invert `y`, after any zeroing.
- **f** — the operation: 1 adds the two conditioned inputs, 0 ands them.
- **no** — invert the answer on the way out.

Every one of those six is a choice between two values, which is the chip you
built in 1.05. So the ALU is layers of muxes with one adder and one and gate
in the middle of them, and nothing in it is new.

Try the bits on paper before wiring anything. Set `zx` and `zy` and leave the
rest at zero: both inputs become zero, and zero added to zero — or anded with
it — is zero, so the chip produces the constant 0 without a constant anywhere
in it. Then work out for yourself which settings give you `x - y`; it is a
satisfying thing to find, and stage 2.05 is the hint.

Two extra outputs matter as much as the number itself. `zr` says the result
was zero; `ng` says it was negative. These are the only things your machine
will ever know about a computation, and every conditional in every program
will be decided by them. Notice that `ng` is free — under two’s complement the
sign is just the top bit — and that `zr` is the Or16Way from 1.08 with a Not
on the end, which is why you built it.

::: watch
This is the largest chip so far and the most punishing to debug later. Test
*every* control combination you intend to use, with at least one negative
operand. An hour of test vectors here saves a week in Part IV.
:::
