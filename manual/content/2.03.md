title: Add16

Sixteen full adders in a row, each one’s carry-out feeding the next one’s
carry-in. Now your machine can add two numbers, which — with the negation
trick two stages from here — means it can also subtract, compare, and count.

What happens to the carry off the far end is your decision to make. Most
designs simply drop it and let numbers wrap around; that is what “overflow”
means and it is a perfectly respectable choice.

This design is called *ripple carry* because the carry has to walk the whole
width before the answer is final. It is the slowest adder anyone builds and it
is the right one to build first. If you later want it faster, the search term
is *carry-lookahead*, and swapping it in is one command once your test vectors
exist.

::: watch
Test the carry chain deliberately: something plus one that ripples the whole
way, and the largest possible pair, which should wrap to a small number.
:::
