The decoder converts one 16-bit instruction into the control signals the rest
of the CPU consumes. With the encoding in 4.02 it is seven gates, because the
instruction bits already *are* those signals. Its only real work is
suppressing them when `op` is 0.

`op` is `instruction[15]`.

```truth
.output | .source bit | .when `op` = 0
`zx nx zy ny f no` | `instruction[11..6]` | pass through unchanged
`useM` | `instruction[12]` | pass through unchanged
`loadA` | `instruction[5]` | forced to `1`
`loadD` | `instruction[4]` | forced to `0`
`writeM` | `instruction[3]` | forced to `0`
`j1 j2 j3` | `instruction[2..0]` | forced to `000`
```

## Where the A-instruction's value goes

Not through this chip. `Decode` has no 15-bit output, and it needs none: the
constant travels from the `instruction` bus straight to the `A` register's
input, on a path built in 4.06. The decoder's entire involvement with an
A-instruction is to stop the rest of the CPU acting on it.

That is necessary because the 16 bits of an instruction are on every one of
these wires at once. Bit 4 is bit 4 whether you call it part of a constant or
`d2`; nothing in the hardware separates the two readings. Left ungated, `@25`
would execute as a C-instruction whose fields are the bits of 25.

`@25` is `0000000000011001`, so `comp` is `000000`, which the ALU reads as
`D&A`:

```truth
.output | .bit | .value | .consequence
`loadA` | 5 | 0 | `A` is not loaded — the instruction fails at its only job
`loadD` | 4 | 1 | `D` is overwritten with `D&A`
`writeM` | 3 | 1 | `D&A` is written to `RAM[A]`
`j3` | 0 | 1 | jumps if `D&A` is positive
```

Gating is not tidiness. It is what makes the A-instruction exist.

## The seven pass-through wires

`zx` through `no` and `useM` need no gates. On an A-instruction they still
carry the constant's bits, so the ALU computes an arbitrary value from `D` and
`A`. That result is harmless because the four gated outputs below are zero:
nothing stores it and nothing jumps on it. The ALU's output goes nowhere.

## The four gated outputs

`loadD`, `writeM` and the three jump bits are suppressed by conjunction with
`op`:

```
loadD  = And(op, instruction[4])
writeM = And(op, instruction[3])
j1     = And(op, instruction[2])
j2     = And(op, instruction[1])
j3     = And(op, instruction[0])
```

`loadA` is the exception, and it is forced the *other* way. Loading `A` is the
entire effect of an A-instruction, so `loadA` must be 1 whenever `op` is 0,
and `instruction[5]` otherwise:

```
loadA = Or(Not(op), instruction[5])
```

One Not, one Or, five Ands. A larger decoder means the encoding has been
changed, not that the chip is hard.

::: watch
Wiring `loadA` as `And(op, instruction[5])` by symmetry with the other three
produces a machine where `@5` has no effect. The symptom appears at the `A`
register — it never changes — so the search starts in the wrong chip.
:::
