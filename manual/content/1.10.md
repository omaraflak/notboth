The routing counterpart, and your address decoder. Two bits in, one of four
lines raised and three held down; then three bits and one of eight.

When you build RAM you will hand it an address and expect exactly one cell to
react. This chip is what makes that true. Build it the same way as the wide
mux: in layers, out of the part you already have.

One thing worth being explicit about, because the pair looks lopsided next to
each other: the muxes in the stage above are sixteen bits wide and these are
one bit. That is not an omission. A mux carries *data* — it chooses between
whole numbers, so it is as wide as a number. A demux carries a *decision* — it
routes a single write signal to one destination out of several. There is no
DMux4Way16 because nothing in the machine needs to send a whole word down one
of four roads; it needs to tell one register out of four to listen.
