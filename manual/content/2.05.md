You need negative numbers, and you do not want to build a second machine for subtracting. The convention that saves you is *two's complement*: negate a number by inverting every bit and adding one. Under that convention `a - b` is `a + (not b)`, therefore the adder you already have is enough.

Here is `5` being negated, and then added back to itself:

```truth
.step | bits | value
start with 5 | 0000000000000101 | 5
invert every bit | 1111111111111010 | −6
add one | 1111111111111011 | −5
add the 5 back | 0000000000000000 | 0
```

The last row carries a `1` off the top end. Since there are only sixteen bits, the seventeenth has nowhere to go and discarding it is exactly what makes the arithmetic wrap round and come out right (i.e. `5 + (-5) = 0`).
