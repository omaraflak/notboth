title: The latch
sig: SRLatch(set, reset) → q
needs: 3.01
---

The first circuit that remembers, and it is two gates wide. Pulse one input
and it locks into one state; pulse the other and it locks into the opposite;
release both and it stays where it was.

- **set**, **reset** — the two commands. **Active low**: idle is 1, and you
  assert one by pulling it to 0.
- **q** — the stored bit. The other gate carries its opposite, at no extra
  cost.

Build it from two **NANDs**, cross-coupled: each gate’s output into one input
of the other. That leaves one input free on each, and those two are `set` and
`reset`.

NAND because the job needs two properties at once. The gate must *invert*, so
the loop’s two nodes hold opposite values — that pair of opposites is what
gives the circuit two states to sit in rather than one, and it is what the And
in 3.01 could not provide. And it must have a *spare input*, which is the only
way to reach in and command it; a Not inverts but leaves nowhere to put the
instruction. Nor has both properties too, and gives the same latch with every
level inverted. Nothing else you have built does.

Active low follows from the same property: a NAND with any input at 0 goes
high whatever else is happening, so 0 is the only thing that can overrule the
loop, and 1 leaves the gate listening to its partner.

```wave
!Timing diagram for the SR latch
set      1111111100111100
reset    1100111111111100
>q       ??00000011111111
>other   ??11111100000011
~0-1 unknown at power-on
~4-7 holds
~10-13 holds
~14-15 contradiction
```

The right-hand end of the picture is the first of two flaws: assert both
inputs and `q` and its opposite go high together, which is a contradiction
rather than a stored bit. The second is what happens on the way out of that
state — release both at once and the two gates are perfectly symmetric with no
way to choose, which shows up here as an oscillation. Real hardware breaks the
tie with manufacturing imperfection; you will break it with a reset. Both
flaws are why the next two stages exist.

::: watch
If the status bar says the circuit is oscillating, that may well be correct
behaviour rather than a bug. Set one input before you switch on.
:::
