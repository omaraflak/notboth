Do not wire anything in this Part until you have written down your instruction
set. The hardware is a *consequence* of the instruction set, not the other way
round, and every hour spent here saves several later.

What you are deciding: how wide an instruction is, what an instruction can
say, and how few kinds of instruction you can get away with. Fewer is
dramatically better — every additional instruction format is more decoding
logic and more ways to be wrong.

One proven shape, worth understanding before you invent your own: two
instruction types, distinguished by a single bit. One loads a constant into a
register. The other says “compute something, put the answer somewhere, and
maybe jump” — with separate little fields for *what to compute*, *where to put
it*, and *when to jump*. Two formats is enough for a real computer, and every
format you add is more decoding logic in 4.04.
