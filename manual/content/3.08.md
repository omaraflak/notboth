num: 3.08
title: Program counter
sig: PC(in[16], load, inc, reset, clk) → out[16]
needs: 3.06, 2.04, 1.05
---

The register that says which instruction to run next, and the last piece of
Part III. Four things can happen to it on a clock edge, and naming all four is
most of the specification:

- **reset** — `out` becomes 0. What happens when you switch the machine on.
- **load** — `out` becomes `in`. A jump, and therefore where every `if`,
  every `while` and every function call eventually comes from.
- **inc** — `out` becomes `out + 1`. The next instruction, which is what
  happens on almost every tick a machine ever runs.
- **none of them asserted** — `out` does not change. This one is easy to
  leave out, because it is the case where nothing is supposed to happen, and
  leaving it out is the usual way to end up with a counter that will not sit
  still.

When more than one is asserted, take them in the order above: reset beats
load, and load beats inc. Any consistent order can be made to work, but that
one agrees with what the words mean — a machine being switched on should not
also be jumping, and an instruction that says “jump” should not also step past
the place it jumped to. Whichever you choose, write it down, because Part IV
will depend on it.

One thing about the shape before you draw it: **the incrementer reads `out`,
not `in`.** A counter counts *itself*. Feed the adder from `in` and you have
built a chip that computes one more than whatever is being loaded, which
accumulates nothing and sits at the same number for ever. It is the same
arrangement as 3.05 one size up: the register’s own output goes back round
into the thing that decides its next input, and that loop is the whole of what
makes it a counter rather than a register.

With that settled it is a register with a stack of muxes in front of it, one
per behaviour, and stage 1.05 paying for itself yet again.

::: watch
All three controls have to make the register *store*, not just choose a value.
Wire the register’s load to the chip’s `load` alone and `inc` and `reset` will
compute exactly the right number and then throw it away, which looks like a
broken incrementer and is not one. One way out: store on every edge, and let
the muxes hand the register its own output when nothing is asserted — then “do
not change” needs no gates of its own.
:::
